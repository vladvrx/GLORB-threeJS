# Glorb hub

The existing intro conversation now arrives in the hub. The hub is a walkable garden island with palms, rocks, benches, sandy paths, and a mint entrance to Level One. The current survival paint square is Level One.

During the opening conversation, the ship and its attached red Glorb hover 24 world units above their original position. The descent, dialogue, and exit camera endpoints share that offset. The aerial opening, sea level, and hub spawn remain unchanged.

The original UFO mesh uses a dedicated cream ceramic, jade, and gold material with panel seams and clear glazing. It uses local mesh coordinates rather than the scenery atlas, with one material and no extra texture downloads. The flight/loading transition uses that same ship finish over neon green water. The intro and hub keep their coastal sea palette.

The purple second shrine opens **Temple Dash**, an endless runner with a 50-coin mission. Walk into that shrine or select **02 / Temple Dash**. A short camera sweep introduces the causeway, then the run starts automatically. Distance stays frozen during the entry cinematic. Run along three lanes, gather coins, jump gold barriers, slide under purple beams, and dodge stone blocks. Speed rises from 12 to 26 world units per second with distance and coin progress. Later rows arrive closer together and can block two lanes, always leaving a reachable clear lane. At 50 coins, a brief mission-complete message and chime celebrate the milestone while the game keeps running. Coins and distance continue increasing until a collision or manual pause.

Swipe anywhere on the playfield: left or right changes lanes, up jumps, and down slides. A/D, Space, S, arrow keys, and the four on-screen buttons also work. Pause with the pause button or Escape. Escape also pauses the entry cinematic; Continue resumes its remaining camera sweep. The phone-focused chase camera sits 6.2 units behind Glorb and 3.7 units above the track, follows lane changes, banks through dodges, and responds to jumps and slides. The persistent rules banner is omitted during play so the track remains clear. Distance plus 25 points per coin gives the score; the best completed score is saved locally. Retry or return to the hub after a collision. Retry starts running immediately. Survival progress stays paused during Temple Dash.

Coin pickups produce gold sparkles and rising chimes. Jump, slide, landing, start, and collision sounds are synthesized through the existing game audio context. Landing dust and impact fragments share the fixed 96-particle pool, adding at most one draw call. The runner sound button shares the hub mute setting. Pausing freezes particles and silences effects; returning to the hub releases their geometry and audio nodes. Reduced-motion preferences shorten entry to a fade and disable camera orbit and banking.

Walk through the entrance or select **Enter Level One**, then **Let's paint**. Use **Hub** to return. Level One's timer, health, paint, upgrades, and player position are retained during hub visits within the current session. Reloading does not save a run.

The hub uses the original responsive island camera. Returning from Temple Dash restores the hub camera and field of view. The raised survival view starts with gameplay and returns when continuing a run. Resizing preserves the camera appropriate to the current area.

The scenery uses a shared coastal palette: turquoise water, pale sky, a black exposed hub floor, warm stone, green foliage, and mint/violet shrine accents. Grass, garden paths, and shrine landings retain their original colors. `hub-visuals.js` batches carved shrines, ribbed palms, flower beds, paving, furniture, and grass detail into one static mesh. Lightweight collision volumes follow the solid rocks, shrubs, palm trunks, benches, lanterns, and shrine pillars while paths, grass detail, flowers, and shrine entrances remain walkable. `runner-visuals.js` recycles detailed causeway sections and uses fixed geometry buffers for coins and obstacles. Survival pod sockets and shrine plinths share one detail mesh. The Kenney Tower Defense Kit was reviewed for shapes and palette; the shipped scenery is original procedural geometry with no new external textures or models.

Hub scenery totals 33,060 triangles in one draw call, including 2,866 triangles of grass detail. Temple scenery totals 19,152 triangles, with all temple objects using at most 11 calls; active particles add at most one call and 384 triangles. Whole-scene counts vary with visible objects and the existing adaptive renderer changes pixel ratio. The desktop runner audit dropped from a 240-call baseline to 17 calls before particles appeared. This is rendering-cost evidence, not a physical-phone FPS benchmark. Run `node tests/profile-world.mjs` to repeat the audit.

`three-js/src/hub.js` adds a scenery group to the recovered scene, sharing its existing floor and player controller. Entering Level One hides the group and restores the square. Existing GLBs, intro dialogue, and world data are not rewritten by the hub. Scenery is decorative; the original floor supplies collision.

Run `node tests/verify-hub.mjs` with the local server on port 43220, or set `GLORB_URL`. This checks the public intro, hub movement, paused resources, Level One entry, returning and continuing, camera changes, resizing, and walking into the entrance. Screenshots are written to `test-results-hub`.

`node --test tests/runner-model.test.mjs` checks collisions, dodging, scoring, pause, generation and bounded obstacle storage. `node tests/verify-runner.mjs` checks physical shrine entry, automatic cinematic entry and pause/resume, camera motion and hub field-of-view restoration, then jumping, sliding, dodging, scoring, collision and retry through browser controls. It writes screenshots to `test-results-runner`.

`node tests/verify-runner-feedback.mjs` checks real coin pickups at 320 by 568, scheduled sound tones and mute behavior, visible particles, pause/cleanup, reduced-motion entry, and pausing on phone rotation. Set `GLORB_URL` to test an extracted package instead of the working source.

`node tests/verify-runner-mission.mjs` collects beyond 50 coins with normal browser inputs, checks the increasing speed and tighter obstacle rows, and verifies that the milestone does not interrupt gameplay before returning to the hub.
